# Examples: Mathegenic Writing Coach in Action

## Example 1: Parent Scenario — "My child can't start writing"

**User input**: "My 11-year-old son has to write a story for school and he's been staring at a blank page for an hour. He asked me if he can use ChatGPT to write it. What should I do?"

**Diagnosis**:
- Role: Parent
- Stage: Structured Awakening (~9-13, restructuring dip)
- Goal: Help child start a narrative story without AI ghostwriting

**Skill response**:

First, validate the child's struggle: "Tell your son that professional writers also stare at blank pages. It means his brain is working."

Then, provide a three-step scaffold:

**Step 1: Draw before writing (10 min)**
Have your son draw a simple map of the story world. It doesn't need to be artistic — just "Where does the story start? Where does it go? Where does it end?" This converts the abstract task "write a story" into the concrete task "design a space."

**Step 2: Use AI as a question-asker, not a writer (5 min)**
Copy this prompt into ChatGPT together with your son:

```
My son is writing a story. He has drawn a map of [describe the map].
Please do NOT write the story for him.
Instead, ask him ONE question at a time to help him think:
1. Who is the main character and what do they want?
2. What is one place on the map where something surprising could happen?
3. If the main character reaches the end, how are they different from when they started?

Ask the first question now. Wait for his answer before asking the next.
```

**Step 3: Write one section at a time (15 min)**
Tell your son: "You only need to write the beginning today. One paragraph." Lower the threshold. Tomorrow, write the middle. The day after, the end.

**Parent talk script for this situation**:
- "Let's not worry about the whole story. What's one tiny scene you can picture?"
- "If your main character were standing right here, what would they be looking at?"
- "You don't need to write well yet. You just need to write what you see."

## Example 2: Teacher Scenario — "Design a lesson based on a textbook text"

**User input**: "I'm a middle school Chinese teacher. I want to use your framework to teach 'A Drop of Water Passes Through Lijiang.' Can you give me a complete lesson plan with AI prompts and worksheets?"

**Diagnosis**:
- Role: Teacher
- Stage: Structured Awakening (~9-13, textbook-based writing)
- Goal: Convert textbook reading into AI-scaffolded travelogue writing

**Skill response**:

Provide the complete **Workflow Plan** + matching **Prompt Cards** + **Worksheets**:

### Workflow: "Borrowing a Special Pair of Eyes" (3-4 class periods)

**Phase 1: Discover the Text's Mechanism (1 period)**
- Student reads the text.
- In pairs, students use Prompt 1.11 (Text Writing Discovery) with AI to uncover: How does the water perspective work? What is the route? What details make it vivid?
- Output: Each pair fills out a "Writing Mechanism Map" worksheet.

**Phase 2: Design Your Own Perspective and Route (1 period)**
- Student chooses a location (school, hometown park, old street) and uses Prompt 1.12 (Perspective Generator) to get 8 perspective options.
- Student selects one perspective and uses Worksheet 2.1 (Route Design Card) to plan the route.
- AI check: Use Prompt 1.13 (Route Design) to validate that the route has emotional progression, not just location changes.

**Phase 3: Language Pattern Lab (0.5 period)**
- Teacher introduces 3 sentence patterns from the text.
- Students write 2 sentences per pattern.
- Use Prompt 1.15 (Language Pattern Lab) to generate additional examples for comparison.
- Output: Worksheet 2.4 (Language Pattern Experiment Sheet).

**Phase 4: Text Roaming and Material Gathering (0.5-1 period)**
- Student uses Prompt 1.16 (Text Roaming Engine) to "walk through" their designed route.
- AI asks observational questions at each stop.
- Student records answers as raw material.
- Output: "Roaming Log" document.

**Phase 5: Structure and First Draft (1 period)**
- Student uses Worksheet 2.3 (Story Structure Table) to organize the roaming log into a narrative structure.
- AI check: Use Prompt 1.17 (Structure Mirror) to identify gaps.
- Student writes first draft independently.

**Phase 6: Feedback and Revision (homework)**
- Student uses Prompt 1.8 (Reflective Reader Feedback) to get AI reader comments.
- Student fills out Worksheet 2.5 (Revision Explanation Sheet).
- Student submits final draft + all process materials.

**Evaluation**: Use Evaluation Tool 3.2 (Project Assessment Dimensions) — 20% structure, 15% language exploration, 20% plot logic, 15% character awareness, 15% AI usage quality, 15% revision reflection.

## Example 3: Advanced User Scenario — "Convert any text into an AI scaffold"

**User input**: "I really like your 'A Drop of Water' case. Can you show me how to apply the same logic to 'From Baicao Garden to Sanwei Study Room' (from the textbook)? I want the full scaffold."

**Diagnosis**:
- Role: Teacher
- Stage: Structured Awakening (textbook conversion)
- Goal: Apply the 8-scaffold model to a different textbook text

**Skill response**:

First, analyze the source text:
- **Text**: "From Baicao Garden to Sanwei Study Room" (Lu Xun)
- **Core writing mechanism**: Spatial transition + contrast between two worlds (freedom vs. discipline) + childhood memory through adult eyes
- **Key structural feature**: The text is organized by space (Baicao → Sanwei), but the emotional core is the contrast between the two spaces.

Then, adapt the 8 scaffolds:

**Scaffold 1 — Text Writing Discovery**:
Change the focus from "water perspective + route" to "spatial contrast + memory tone."
Key questions: How does Lu Xun make Baicao Garden feel like a paradise? What specific details create the feeling of "freedom"? How does the tone shift when entering Sanwei Study?

**Scaffold 2 — Perspective Generator**:
Instead of "non-human narrator," the perspectives could be:
- A childhood object (toy, insect, plant) that witnessed both spaces
- An adult memory returning to the place
- A sound (laughter, reading voice) that echoes in both spaces

**Scaffold 3 — Route Design**:
Instead of a "travel route," design a "contrast route": Space A (free) → Transition → Space B (structured). Each stop must highlight a contrast dimension (sights, sounds, activities, feelings).

**Scaffold 4 — Detail Amplification**:
Focus on the five senses, but with an emphasis on how the SAME sense changes between the two spaces. For example: "What sounds did you hear in Baicao? What sounds in Sanwei? How does the change in sound reflect the change in feeling?"

**Scaffold 5 — Language Pattern Lab**:
Patterns based on Lu Xun's style:
- Pattern: "In [Place A], I could [free action], but in [Place B], I could only [restricted action]."
- Pattern: "I thought [Place B] would be [negative expectation], but actually it was [surprising nuance]."

**Scaffold 6 — Text Roaming**:
AI guides the student through their own two spaces, asking: "What is the Baicao-equivalent in your life? What is the Sanwei-equivalent? What happened in the transition?"

**Scaffold 7 — Structure Mirror**:
Check: Does the contrast between the two spaces become sharper as the text progresses? Is there a moment where the narrator realizes something about the second space that wasn't obvious at first?

**Scaffold 8 — Revision Explanation**:
Same as original.

**Final output**: A complete 8-scaffold package customized for the new text, with all prompts rewritten to match the text's specific writing mechanism.
